Eight handclap modes, two rooms. Spectra and raw waveforms of recorded handclaps (left) and their inferred RIRs (right). Waveforms retain the original digital amplitudes.

From a handclap to a room response

Handclaps offer an equipment-free excitation for room acoustics, but the source waveform varies with the person and hand configuration. We study supervised estimation of a one-second room impulse response (RIR) from a reverberant handclap, using controlled training pairs made with recorded anechoic handclaps and RIRs.

This page provides additional real phone-recording examples for listening and visual inspection. The phone recordings have no paired reference RIR, so these examples illustrate model behavior and do not establish reconstruction accuracy.

Listen: phone claps and inferred RIRs

iPhone 15 recordings from seven rooms, originally at 48 kHz and resampled to 44.1 kHz. Every room contains eight protocol modes with five repetitions each. The default example for each room and mode is the existing unclipped recording with the highest pre-handclap SNR, selected using only the input.

Both players use the original one-second model interval. Each WAV is independently peak-normalized to −1 dBFS for listening only; no denoising or filtering is applied. Listen for the onset and decay of each inferred response, and compare the two rooms. There is no paired reference RIR.

Spectra and temporal RMS envelopes

The temporal panels show 0–250 ms RMS envelopes, with a 5 ms window and approximately 1 ms spacing. They use the original digital amplitudes without peak normalization, with shared −60 to 0 dBFS limits and digital full scale equal to one for every curve.

These combined figures use 0.1–15 kHz spectra from the first 0.9 s, with 1/12-octave power averaging and the original per-signal mean FFT-bin power normalization over 0.1–10 kHz. All spectral panels share −40 to +20 dB limits. Only the temporal panels use unnormalized digital amplitudes; these are not calibrated sound-pressure levels.

Anechoic handclap examples

The source handclaps were recorded with eight microphones in the large anechoic chamber at Aalto University. Below are five retained examples from different participants. These listening files preserve each complete retained event on the primary microphone channel.

Participant IDs are kept for provenance. We do not assign P1/A1 or other pose labels to these individual anechoic events, because their retained-event metadata do not establish those labels.
